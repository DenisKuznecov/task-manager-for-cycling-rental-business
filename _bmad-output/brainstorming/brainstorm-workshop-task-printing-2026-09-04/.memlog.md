- (decision) Facilitator mode; batch: Yes And Building, First Principles Thinking, Starbursting, How Might We.
- (technique) started Yes And Building — never negate; build each response from the preceding idea.
- (idea) The M1 printout makes the next mechanic see that a bike needs re-checking; M2 then opens the app and works on that bike.
- (idea) The M1 re-check paper shows a prominent large number 1 to signal re-check, followed by the order number, bike name, and stock ID.
- (decision) The large number 1 is sufficient to distinguish a re-check tag from other workshop paper; no additional visual marker is needed.
- (decision) M2 throws away the M1 re-check tag when starting work; no app action tracks or retires that paper.
- (idea) The M2 replacement sheet hangs on the bike until customer handover, signaling to everyone in the shop that the bike is ready for pickup.
- (direction) Kickoff requirement: M1 presses an app button when bike prep is ready for re-check, automatically printing a paper placed on the bike.
- (direction) Kickoff requirement: M2 prints a customer-facing replacement sheet with logo, full completed checklist, M1 and M2 check marks, both mechanic names, and preparation timestamp; it remains on the bike until handover.
- (direction) Kickoff technical context: printer identified as Epson TM-m30II and already prints order receipts; seek network/IP printing without manual Bluetooth connection from the app device.
- (idea) The final customer-facing sheet should show a logo, completed checklist, and mechanic signatures.
- (technique) started First Principles Thinking — strip assumptions to bedrock facts and rebuild from truth alone.
- (insight) Bedrock fact after M1 finishes: the bike is prepared after M1.
- (insight) The bike is finished only when M2 completes their work and hangs the final paper on the bike.
- (decision) M2 may print and hang the final paper only after completing every checklist item shown for M2; until then the task/bike is not prepared.
- (decision) M1 may print the re-check tag only after completing every checklist item shown for M1.
- (technique) started Starbursting — interrogate the idea with who, what, where, when, why, and how questions.
- (decision) A printer failure must not block a mechanic from advancing a task after their required checklist items are complete.
- (idea) Mechanics can visually confirm printing because the printer is in front of them; the app should also show an error when a print attempt fails.
- (idea) Keep the print buttons available on the task so either paper can be reprinted when a copy is corrupted, lost, or a prior attempt failed.
- (decision) The final customer-sheet print action is disabled until M2 completes every required re-check item; after completion it remains available for reprinting.
- (decision) The re-check-tag print action is disabled until M1 completes every required item; after completion it remains available for reprinting throughout the rest of the task.
- (technique) started How Might We — reframe the problem as opportunity questions before ideating against the sharpest one.
- (question) How might we improve bike preparation and re-check visibility in the workshop while giving customers a nice-looking paper showing that the bike was thoroughly prepared?
- (direction) The paper attached to the bike is itself the workshop notice that the bike is waiting for re-check; treat this as settled and do not reopen alternative visibility mechanisms.
- (direction) Remaining areas to explore are how the app connects to the Epson printer and how the re-check and customer-facing papers should look.
- (decision) Normal printing UX is one tap: the paper prints immediately, the mechanic takes it, and attaches it to the bike; there are no additional mechanic-facing steps.
- (insight) Printer setup cannot be product-brainstormed without technical knowledge; treat the Epson TM-m30II connection method as a focused feasibility research item.
- (insight) The final sheet should make the customer feel that exceptional, thorough work was done and that the company is highly professional.
- (decision) The final sheet is designed for monochrome thermal printing; a logo is optional if it renders well, while the completed checklist is the primary professional-looking content.
- (idea) Make the final checklist feel deliberately designed through strong text structure and clearly displayed mechanic names.
- (idea) Final-sheet order: checklist items with check marks, followed by a Bike prepared by line and a Bike re-checked by line.
- (question) Clarify whether Bike prepared by should name M1 or M2; the latest response named M2 for both lines, while the kickoff called for both mechanics.
- (decision) The final sheet ends with Bike prepared by [M1 name] and Bike re-checked by [M2 name].
- (decision) MoSCoW convergence: all ten identified product, UX, resilience, technical-investigation, visual-design, and physical-paper rules are Musts for the first release.
- (insight) The feature is one coherent system: each paper is a physical projection of a digitally verified task state—M1 paper means ready for M2, M2 paper means ready for customer—while the app remains the source of truth.
- (insight) Checklist gating keeps printed claims truthful; persistent reprint actions and non-blocking failures keep disposable paper and printer reliability from controlling task state.
- (insight) Convergence conclusion: every identified element matters because the value comes from the complete operational and customer-facing chain, not an isolated print action.
