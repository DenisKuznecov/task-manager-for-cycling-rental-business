- (insight by user) Current integration ingests a flat order-updated webhook, extracts the Booqable order ID, then fetches the full order and related customer, bike, and accessory data before saving selected customer data locally
- (insight by user) There is no ground-zero reconciliation that mirrors all Booqable orders, customers, inventory, coupons, and related records into the local database
- (insight by user) Workshop-task design is weakest around knowing stable item identity, detecting item updates, and avoiding fragile item-title parsing
- (idea by user) Import all relevant Booqable data initially, then maintain per-entity create, update, and delete synchronization through webhooks so local IDs and relationships can drive workshop-task rules
- (question by user) Could a synchronized local model simplify workshop-task reactions to Booqable order and configuration changes?
- (question by user) Would a full mirror exceed Supabase and Vercel free-tier capacity or operational limits?
- (question by user) Would permanent synchronization couple the product too tightly to Booqable and create excessive maintenance?
- (question by user) Is this architectural change large enough to require its own brainstorm, PRD, UX, architecture, and implementation lifecycle?
- (direction by user) Keep this exploration conceptual and do not modify existing workshop-task PRD or UX artifacts unless the option is later approved
- (technique by coach) batch order: Ecosystem Thinking, Constraint Mapping, TRIZ Contradiction, Scenario Cross
- (technique by coach) started Ecosystem Thinking
- (decision by user) Target a selective warehouse rather than a full Booqable mirror; Booqable and the app share only the main building blocks needed by local workflows
- (idea by user) Candidate synchronized entities are orders, customers, inventory bundles, individual inventory items, coupon codes, physical bikes, and bike identification numbers for history tracking
- (idea by coach) Require every warehouse entity to earn admission through a concrete local workflow dependency, preventing convenient-but-unused Booqable data from expanding scope
- (insight by user) Local shared entities enable partner-order organization, workshop tasks, physical-bike change history, bike-to-order history, and customer order and purchase history
- (insight by coach) The named workflows directly justify orders, customers, inventory, physical bikes, and bike identifiers, but do not yet establish a local dependency on coupon-code entities
- (insight by user) Partner attribution is derived from the promo code used on a Booqable order, so coupon information participates in the partner-order workflow
- (idea by coach) A thinner coupon projection may store only each encountered order promo-code identity plus its local partner mapping instead of synchronizing the entire Booqable coupon catalogue
- (decision by user) Do not synchronize the full promo-code catalogue for current needs; store promo codes used for partner attribution and retain each partner's historical codes as they change over years
- (insight by coach) Partner promo-code history is product-owned relationship data that references Booqable codes, not necessarily a mirrored Booqable coupon entity
- (idea by coach) Model partner promo codes as a local historical mapping with effective periods or active state, while orders preserve the exact code used at booking time
- (direction by user) Current partner promo-code workflow already exists in the partner feature
- (insight by coach) Current implementation stores one unique partners.promo_code and order sync matches coupon/custom-field candidates against it; historical partner promo-code mappings are not currently modeled
- (idea by coach) Keep coupon-catalogue synchronization outside the selective warehouse and treat multi-code partner history as a separate small extension to the existing partner model
- (decision by user) Exclude coupon-catalogue synchronization and partner promo-code history from this scope
- (idea by coach) Separate the ecosystem into read-only Booqable projections, product-owned workflow records, and stable links between them to contain coupling and avoid two-way synchronization conflicts
- (decision by user) Booqable remains the authoritative source for order changes, customer management, and inventory CRUD; the local warehouse is one-way and read-only for projected source fields
- (insight by coach) A one-way authority boundary avoids write conflicts and materially reduces synchronization complexity compared with a bidirectional integration
- (idea by coach) Represent Booqable deletions as local tombstones instead of hard deletes when historical orders, workshop work, or physical-bike history still references the entity
- (question by user) Clarify whether preserving deleted Booqable records means displaying an Archived label beside deleted customers, bikes, or inventory items
- (insight by coach) Deletion retention differs by entity: inventory and bikes can keep archived identity snapshots for history, while deleted customers may require PII anonymization even when order history remains
- (decision by user) Use archival rather than hard deletion for synchronized Booqable entities so historical references remain intact
- (technique by coach) started Constraint Mapping
- (insight by user) Current Booqable scale is approximately 350 orders, 250 customers, 63 product definitions across rentals and services, and 13 bundles
- (insight by coach) Current record volume is small enough that Supabase database storage is unlikely to be the limiting constraint; synchronization completeness, API pagination, webhook coverage, and Vercel execution duration are more material
- (insight by user) Booqable webhook documentation exposes create/update/archive events for several required resources, including bundles, bundle items, customers, orders, and product groups
- (insight by coach) The documented event list shown does not clearly include physical stock-item events and appears incomplete for product update/archive, so those entity paths require technical verification
- (insight by coach) Initial population is one-time, but reliable synchronization still needs recurring reconciliation because webhooks are change signals and can be missed, delayed, duplicated, or delivered out of order
- (idea by coach) Build one idempotent paginated backfill/reconciliation CLI that upserts by Booqable ID, checkpoints progress, retries safely, reports counts, and can target local, staging, or production explicitly
- (idea by coach) Develop and validate the importer locally, then run the same data-only job for staging and production through a manually dispatched GitHub Action rather than a Vercel request or an unlogged laptop-only procedure
- (idea by coach) Keep webhook requests short by recording each event durably and synchronizing only the affected entity, while periodic reconciliation repairs anything missed
- (insight by user) Current webhook handling treats payloads only as change notifications and fetches canonical entity data from Booqable because webhook payload structure is poor
- (insight by user) Local-ngrok and production order webhook subscriptions were created through ad hoc terminal commands and have no repository-owned subscription record
- (question by user) How should entities without webhook coverage be kept current instead of remaining stale indefinitely?
- (idea by coach) Retain fetch-after-webhook: use entity type and ID as the signal, fetch the latest canonical Booqable representation, then idempotently upsert or archive only the affected local projection
- (idea by coach) Use layered freshness: webhooks for near-real-time changes, scheduled paginated reconciliation for missed or unsupported events, and just-in-time order refresh before consequential workshop transitions
- (idea by coach) Create a repository-owned environment-specific webhook subscription manifest and repeatable setup/audit script, keeping secret URLs in environment variables rather than documentation
- (decision by user) Nightly reconciliation plus just-in-time order refresh is provisionally fresh enough for workshop operations
- (idea by coach) Keep full reconciliation outside Vercel while each webhook performs only a bounded canonical fetch and targeted upsert, containing the 10-second runtime risk
- (idea by coach) Expose minimal synchronization health: last successful run per entity, failed event count, error details, and a manual retry action so stale data does not remain invisible
- (decision by user) Minimum sync operations UI shows failed webhook events and provides a manual retry action
- (idea by coach) Use a durable webhook event inbox with received, processing, succeeded, and failed states; retain entity identity, attempts, and error context so retry is safe and visible

- (insight by coach) Resolve coupling with mirror identity but own semantics: preserve Booqable IDs and relationships, translate selected source fields through an adapter, and keep local names and workflow meanings stable
- (idea by coach) Normalize inventory into catalog definitions, bundle composition, physical tracked assets, and order-item assignments so workshop logic uses stable IDs rather than item-title parsing
- (question by user) Clarify whether physical-asset history should apply only to bikes or be designed for other tracked inventory
- (decision by user) Per-physical-item history is bike-only; other products may be synchronized as catalog or order context but do not need individual asset histories
- (insight by coach) The selective warehouse can remain narrow by syncing physical stock-item identities only for bikes while representing other products at the catalog and order-line level
